# What to send the owner, and when

Four messages, in order. Written to be sent as they are — short, no jargon, and each one asks for
exactly one thing.

The tone matters more than it looks. He is being asked to let software answer his customers on his
own number. Every one of these is written so he can say no easily, because a man who feels cornered
into yes cancels in month two.

---

## 1. Asking for the visit

Send this as soon as the Meta application is in. Nothing else gates it — templates come later,
after his number is connected. Keep it as one message; a wall of text gets skimmed.

Do not wait for approval before asking for the visit. Verification takes days and the visit takes
thirty minutes; run them at the same time.

> Assalamu alaikum, hope business is good.
>
> I have built something for the garage and it is ready to show you. It answers WhatsApp for you —
> prices, timings, location, and it books cars in. It uses your same number, and you keep using
> your phone exactly like now.
>
> It only says what you tell it to say. It never guesses a price.
>
> Can I come to the workshop for 30 minutes this week? I need your price list and I want to show
> you how it works before anything is connected. No cost, and nothing changes on your phone until
> you say yes.

**Roman Urdu, if that is easier for him:**

> Assalamu alaikum, umeed hai kaam acha chal raha hai.
>
> Maine garage ke liye ek cheez banayi hai, aap ko dikhana chahta hoon. Ye aap ka WhatsApp khud
> answer karta hai — price, timing, location, aur car ki booking bhi le leta hai. Aap ka wohi
> number use karta hai, aur aap apna phone bilkul pehle jaisa hi use karte rahenge.
>
> Ye sirf wohi batata hai jo aap ne likha hua hai. Price kabhi apne se nahi banata.
>
> Is week 30 minute ke liye workshop aa sakta hoon? Mujhe aap ki price list chahiye aur main aap ko
> chala kar dikhana chahta hoon. Koi kharcha nahi, aur jab tak aap haan nahi kehte phone par kuch
> nahi badlega.

**Why it is worded this way**

- *"your same number"* is the whole pitch. Say it in the first three lines.
- *"never guesses a price"* is the objection he has not raised yet. Answer it before he does.
- *"nothing changes until you say yes"* removes the risk from agreeing to a meeting.
- Thirty minutes, not "a quick chat". He is running a business; give him the real number.

## 1b. The pilot terms — say these out loud at the workshop

Not a message. Four sentences, said while he is looking at the demo. They take a minute and they
decide whether the free month turns into a paying customer or just ends.

### Call it a pilot, not a trial

> I want to run this at your garage for a month, free, as a pilot. You are the first one.

A trial makes him a customer evaluating you. A pilot makes him a partner helping build it, and a
partner hands over his price sheet and his chat history without being chased. You need those far
more than you need his first payment.

### Say the price on day one

> It is free for the first month. After that it is AED 400 a month if you want to keep it.

**Never leave the price until day 30.** If you do, he anchors on free and every conversation
afterwards is a negotiation. Saying it now also tells you immediately whether he would ever pay —
watch his face when you say the number. If he flinches, ask what it would need to do to be worth
it, and listen properly; that answer is worth more than the sale.

### Agree how it ends, before it starts

> On day 30 I will show you exactly how many enquiries came in after you closed, and how many
> became bookings. If it is not worth AED 400 to you, we switch it off, no hard feelings.

Three jobs in one sentence: it makes saying no easy, it books you twenty minutes of his attention
on day 30, and it means the decision gets made on numbers rather than on how he feels that morning.

### Ask for something instead of money

> If it works, introduce me to two garage owners you know.

Two warm introductions from a garage owner beat two hundred cold messages, and it costs him nothing
to promise now.

### The catch with free, and how to close it

A man paying nothing does not care when it breaks, does not tell you when a customer complains, and
drifts. Ten minutes of his attention is the real price, so ask for it as part of the deal:

> All I need from you is a quick word on day three if anything has annoyed you, and twenty minutes
> at the end of the month.

### Before any of this — the free test that decides everything

Ask him this first, before you demo anything:

> Can you scroll your WhatsApp back two weeks? How many messages came in after 7pm — and how many
> did you answer the same night?

If it is forty and he answered five, you have a business and he will fund it himself. If it is
three, the premise is wrong for a garage his size and you have learned that for the price of a
coffee. Either way it costs nothing and it is the most useful hour in the whole project.

## 2. The day before connecting

Send the evening before. This one exists because an out-of-date app is the single most common
reason the connection fails, and finding out at the workshop wastes the visit.

> See you tomorrow at [time].
>
> One thing before I come — please open the App Store / Play Store and update WhatsApp Business to
> the newest version. If it is old, the connection will not work and we will waste the visit.
>
> Also please have ready:
> • your personal WhatsApp number (the one you want alerts on, not the garage number)
> • your Google Maps link for the workshop
> • how many cars you can start in one hour on a normal day

## 3. Before he scans the QR code

Not a message — say this out loud, at the workshop, before he taps anything. The full list is in
`ONBOARDING.md`. He must hear it from you rather than discover it later.

> Before we connect it, three things change on your phone. Nothing else does.
>
> One — you will not be able to make new broadcast lists, and old ones become read-only. If you
> send offers that way, tell me now and we will think about it.
>
> Two — disappearing messages and view-once stop working in normal chats.
>
> Three — WhatsApp Web will log out once today. You just scan again after.
>
> Your calls, your groups, your chats, your contacts — all stay exactly as they are. And we can
> disconnect it any time and your number goes back to normal.

If he hesitates on broadcast lists, stop and talk about it. That is the one real cost, and finding
out after the fact is how a pilot dies.

---

## 4. After it is live

Leave this with him, and send it as a message too so he can find it later.

> All set. Two things to know:
>
> If you reply to any customer yourself, I go quiet in that chat automatically. You do not need to
> do anything.
>
> If you ever want me to stop completely, send **/bot off**. To start again, **/bot on**.
> Send **/status** any time and I will send you today's numbers.
>
> You will get a message from me for every booking, and a summary every evening at 6.
>
> Anything odd, call me straight away — [your number].

---

## Then leave him alone

Do not check in every day. Ask once, on day three: *"Has anything annoyed you?"* Fix whatever he
says, and let the report do the talking at the end of the month.
